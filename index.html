Hello and welcome! I am a PhD student at the Resilient Architecture Lab at the University of New Hampshire, where I work under the guidance of Dr. Dean Sullivan, and I expect to graduate in summer 2027. Computing systems rely on layers of hardware and low-level software, and a defect at any layer can undermine everything built on top of it. My work asks how we can find and fix those defects automatically, and how we can tell honestly whether a proposed fix is correct.

My research follows automated program repair across several levels of abstraction, namely register-transfer level (RTL) hardware, high-level synthesis (HLS) code, and firmware. A thread that connects these topics is the evaluation of large language models (LLMs) as repair tools. I care about benchmarks and oracles that measure real progress, so a large part of my work studies where pass rates mislead and what a trustworthy evaluation needs.

Current Projects

  • LLM Memorization in Code Repair. I am testing whether an LLM's repair success reflects real understanding or recall of code it has seen before. The study reruns repair on semantics-preserving variants of each design (renamed, reformatted, and reordered code) and compares the results with the originals.
  • Mining Firmware Security Fixes. I am mining the version history of open-source Bluetooth Low Energy firmware (Zephyr, Apache NimBLE, and MCUboot) to find recurring security fix patterns. The fixes are grouped into bug families, with the goal of turning them into reusable automated repair templates.
  • Mutator Engine for RTL Repair (TGM). TGM is a repair engine that searches for fixes to buggy hardware designs by applying guided mutation operators to the RTL source. It uses fault localization to decide where to mutate and differential simulation to check candidates, so a patch counts as a repair only when it is verified and not merely when it passes the tests.
  • Reliable Oracles for HLS Repair. I am extending my benchmark work on high-level synthesis, with a focus on how trustworthy the test oracles behind LLM repair results really are.
